The problem, in plain terms

Everybody has the knowledge written down. Almost nobody has it modelled.

A client's process already exists on paper — in an SOP, a policy, a training deck, a recorded workshop. Turning that into a process map that a consultant, an automation team or an auditor can actually use is done by hand, one box at a time. It is slow, it is expensive, and the person doing it is a scarce specialist.

Where six hours per process actually goes

Reading and understanding the source 1.2 h
Drafting the steps, roles and systems 1.5 h
Drawing the BPMN — lanes, gateways, layout 2.0 h
Fixing naming and modelling conventions 0.8 h
Review rework after the first pass 0.5 h
Total, per single process 6.0 h

Multiply it. A mid-sized transformation maps 250 processes. That is 1,500 analyst hours — roughly 9 months of one person's working year — spent drawing boxes rather than improving the process inside them.

And the tools that exist do not close the gap

Modelling editors

Visio, Camunda Modeler, Signavio. Excellent drawing surfaces — but they read nothing. Every box is still placed by a human who has already read the document.

Process mining

Celonis, ABBYY, UiPath. Needs an event log from a transactional system. A process that only exists in a document or in someone's head produces no log at all.

Diagram / OCR converters

Recover shapes and text from a picture. A rectangle becomes a rectangle — not a task, owned by a role, conditional on a threshold. The meaning is left behind.

A chat AI, used directly

Produces XML that may not open, carries no layout, and gives the reviewer no way to see what it understood before accepting it — or to fix one step without regenerating everything.

The solution — how it works

Four steps. The third one is the reason it can be trusted.

1

Take it in

Upload the SOP, transcript, image, spreadsheet or Visio file. Nothing is retyped. Inside a Word document it keeps the headings, the numbered clauses, the tables as tables, and any flowchart pasted into the page.

2

Read it

The AI reads the words and the pictures together, so a diagram drawn on page 4 settles the branching that the text left vague. It comes back with steps, roles, systems, documents and decision rules.

3

Show its working

A human checks what it understood — before any diagram is drawn. Steps appear in an editable table. Correct a role, add a step, fix a threshold. Nothing is drawn until you approve.

4

Build and audit

The approved understanding becomes real BPMN 2.0 — pools, lanes, gateways, proper layout — then it is audited against the modelling rulebook and handed back with the gaps listed.

Why step 3 changes everything. Every other AI tool asks you to check its output — a finished diagram, where an error is expensive to find and expensive to fix. DiagramIQ asks you to check its understanding, in a table, in thirty seconds, before a single box exists. Corrections cost one keystroke instead of one re-draw.

What you get back, every time

A diagram that actually opens

BPMN 2.0 with full layout information — it lands in Signavio, Camunda or bpmn.io ready to work on, not as a picture to be redrawn.

A conformance audit

Checked against the structural rules and the house modelling conventions, with each finding named and graded rather than a bare pass or fail.

The questions to ask the business

Where the source never said who approves above a limit, it says so — and gives you the question to put to the client, instead of quietly inventing an answer.

An honest label on the result

If it could only manage a partial reading, it says which parts are missing. It never hands back a lesser result dressed as a complete one.

And it keeps working with you

Describe a correction in ordinary English — "Approve request belongs to Finance, not Procurement" — and it applies it, then tells you exactly what it changed and what it did not. If a revision makes things worse, roll back to the previous version. Export the steps to Excel, edit them in the tool everyone already has, and re-import: the edits patch the existing diagram instead of duplicating it.

USP 1

It reads the picture and the paragraph as one

An SOP explains a rule in words on page 2 and draws it on page 4. Every other tool sees one or the other. DiagramIQ sends both to the model together, positioned where they sat in the document.

USP 2

The human signs off the understanding, not the drawing

Verification happens on a table of steps, before generation. And the diagram itself is built by fixed rules — so the AI's judgement is reviewed, and the AI never gets to write invalid XML.

USP 3

It is honest about what it does not know

Missing thresholds become questions for the business, not invented answers. A partial reading is labelled as partial. That is what makes the output safe to put in front of a client.
